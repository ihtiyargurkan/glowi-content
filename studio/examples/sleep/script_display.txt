Why does your skin look worse after one bad night of sleep?

It's not in your head. When researchers kept people awake for 31 hours, strangers rated their skin paler, their eyes puffier, and their dark circles deeper.

Night is your skin's repair shift. Its cells divide most around midnight.

Skip that shift, and your stress hormone, cortisol, stays high into the next evening. Over time, too much cortisol slows down collagen production.

Fluid also pools under the thin skin around your eyes, and your face loses its color.

In a study of 60 women, good sleepers repaired a damaged skin barrier 30% better than poor sleepers, and showed fewer signs of aging.

So protect the night shift. Aim for 7–9 hours, keep the same bedtime, and put your screen down an hour before sleep.
