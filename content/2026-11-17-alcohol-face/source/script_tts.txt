Why does a night of drinking show up on your face?

Most people blame dehydration. That's only part of the story.

When your body breaks alcohol down, it makes a harsh byproduct. It opens up the tiny blood vessels in your skin, so your face flushes red.

Alcohol also mutes the signal that tells your kidneys to hold water. You lose fluid, and your sleep turns light and broken.

In a survey of three thousand two hundred sixty-seven women in four countries, those who had eight or more drinks a week showed more under-eye puffiness and more visible blood vessels.

It's a link, not proof. But it fits what alcohol does to your vessels and your sleep.

You don't have to quit. Drink less, sip water between glasses, and finish your last drink well before bed.

Your face keeps a record of the weekend. Glowee helps you see the pattern.
