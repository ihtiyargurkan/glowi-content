# Sources — Why does a night of drinking show up on your face?
1. Goodman GD, Kaufman J, Day D, et al. Impact of Smoking and Alcohol Use on Facial Aging in Women: Results of a Large Multinational, Multiracial, Cross-sectional Survey. Plast Reconstr Surg Glob Open. 2019. PMID 31531169. https://read.qxmd.com/read/31531169 (abstract opened): n=3,267 women aged 18–75, US/Australia/Canada/UK; heavy alcohol use defined as ≥8 drinks/week; associated with upper facial lines, under-eye puffiness, oral commissures, midface volume loss and visible blood vessels. Cross-sectional.
2. DermNet NZ — Cutaneous adverse effects of alcohol. https://www.dermnetnz.org/topics/cutaneous-adverse-effects-of-alcohol/ — flushing is due to acetaldehyde (main breakdown product of alcohol) via histamine release; alcohol causes vasodilation and facial flushing; persistent redness linked to enlarged blood vessels.
3. NIAAA — Hangovers fact sheet. https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/brochures-and-fact-sheets/hangovers — "Alcohol suppresses the release of vasopressin" → increased urination and fluid loss; sleep after drinking is fragmented.
