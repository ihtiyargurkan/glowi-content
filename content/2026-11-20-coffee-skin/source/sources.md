# Sources — Does your morning coffee dry out your skin?
1. Killer SC, Blannin AK, Jeukendrup AE. No evidence of dehydration with moderate daily coffee intake: a counterbalanced cross-over study in a free-living population. PLoS One. 2014. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0084154 and https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/641006 : 50 male regular coffee drinkers; 4 × 200 ml coffee a day (4 mg/kg caffeine) vs the same volume of water, 3 days each, crossover; no differences in total body water, blood or urine hydration markers; "caffeine elicits a diuretic effect" in large doses (≥500 mg); habitual consumers may develop tolerance; coffee "provides similar hydrating qualities to water". Funded by ISIC (coffee industry body).
2. Drake C, Roehrs T, Shambroom J, Roth T. Caffeine effects on sleep taken 0, 3, or 6 hours before going to bed. J Clin Sleep Med. 2013. AASM release https://aasm.org/late-afternoon-and-early-evening-caffeine-can-disrupt-sleep-at-night/ : 12 healthy sleepers, 400 mg caffeine; taken 6 hours before bed it reduced objectively measured total sleep by more than one hour. The authors suggest avoiding caffeine at least 6 hours before bed; earlier is better since even 6 h cut sleep.
3. Sundelin T, et al. Cues of fatigue: effects of sleep deprivation on facial appearance. Sleep. 2013. https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/08/130830161323.htm : 10 people photographed after normal sleep vs 31 h awake, rated by 40 people; sleep-deprived faces had paler skin, darker circles, more swollen eyes, hanging eyelids.
