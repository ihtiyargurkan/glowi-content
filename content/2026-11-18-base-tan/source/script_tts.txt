Why doesn't a base tan protect your skin?

Lots of people tan first, hoping to burn less later. The protection is far smaller than it looks.

A tan isn't a shield. It's an alarm. U-V hits the D-N-A inside your skin cells, and that damage tells your pigment cells to make more melanin.

The melanin settles over each cell's core like a tiny umbrella. But the umbrellas are thin, and plenty of light still gets through.

In one study, sixteen volunteers got ten U-V sessions over two weeks. Their tan made their skin at most about two times harder to burn.

That's roughly S-P-F two at best. A sunscreen labeled S-P-F thirty is rated fifteen times higher.

So skip the base tan. Wear broad-spectrum S-P-F thirty or higher every day, and find shade when the sun is strongest.
