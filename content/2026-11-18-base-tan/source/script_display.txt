Why doesn't a base tan protect your skin?

Lots of people tan first, hoping to burn less later. The protection is far smaller than it looks.

A tan isn't a shield. It's an alarm. UV hits the DNA inside your skin cells, and that damage tells your pigment cells to make more melanin.

The melanin settles over each cell's core like a tiny umbrella. But the umbrellas are thin, and plenty of light still gets through.

In one study, 16 volunteers got 10 UV sessions over 2 weeks. Their tan made their skin at most about 2 times harder to burn.

That's roughly SPF 2 at best. A sunscreen labeled SPF 30 is rated 15 times higher.

So skip the base tan. Wear broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher every day, and find shade when the sun is strongest.
