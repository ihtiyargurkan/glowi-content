# Sources — Why doesn't a base tan protect your skin?
1. Sheehan JM, Potten CS, Young AR. Tanning in human skin types II and III offers modest photoprotection against erythema. Photochem Photobiol. 1998;68(4):588–592. doi:10.1111/j.1751-1097.1998.tb02518.x — abstract via https://www.bentham.co.uk/knowledge/tools-resources/papers-citations/article/tanning-in-human-skin-types-ii-and-iii-offers-modest-photoprotection-against-erythema-1059/ : 16 volunteers (8 type II, 8 type III), solar-simulated UV on weekdays for 2 weeks (0.5 or 0.75 MED per session); induced protection factor 1.4–2.1 (type II) and 1.5–2.3 (type III); "offers only moderate protection".
2. Cui R, Widlund HR, Feige E, et al. Central role of p53 in the suntan response and pathologic hyperpigmentation. Cell. 2007 — summarized at https://rupress.org/jcb/article/177/1/5b/34575/Tanning-with-p53 : UV-induced DNA damage up-regulates p53, which drives POMC/MSH so melanocytes make melanin; p53-deficient mice fail to tan.
3. "SPF 30" is a label rating; 30 / 2 = 15 is arithmetic on the study's ~2× figure.
