Why do your pores look bigger as you get older?

Part of it is real. A three-D study of cheek skin found that pores grow larger and longer with age.

Think of skin as a stretchy net. Each pore is a hole, held in shape by a ring of springy collagen and elastic fibers.

Over the years, and faster with sun, that ring loosens. With less support, the round opening sags into a longer oval.

But age isn't the whole story. In two thousand five hundred eighty-five women from five countries, pore size varied much more with ethnicity than with age.

And shine plays tricks. Oil on the surface can make pores look bigger than they are.

So support the net. Wear sunscreen every day, ask about a retinoid, and blot shine. Toners only tighten them for a moment.
