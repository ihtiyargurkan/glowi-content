# Sources — tomato paste and sun protection (opened 2026-11-09)
1. Stahl W, Heinrich U, Wiseman S, Eichler O, Sies H, Tronnier H. Dietary tomato paste protects against ultraviolet light-induced erythema in humans. J Nutr. 2001;131(5):1449-51. PubMed 11340098. Abstract read at https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/82125745
   - 9 volunteers ate 40 g tomato paste (~16 mg lycopene) with 10 g olive oil daily for 10 weeks; 10 controls had olive oil only (19 adults).
   - Erythema induced with a solar simulator at weeks 0, 4 and 10. No significant difference at week 4. At week 10, "dorsal erythema formation was 40% lower in the group that consumed tomato paste" (P = 0.02). Serum lycopene rose.
2. Science News (2001), "Dietary protection against sunburn": https://www.sciencenews.org/blog/food-for-thought/dietary-protection-against-sunburn-recipe
   - Quotes Wilhelm Stahl: protection "comparable to a tanning lotion with a sun-protection factor (SPF) of only 2 or 3."
3. Rizwan M et al. Tomato paste rich in lycopene protects against cutaneous photodamage in humans in vivo: a randomized controlled trial. Br J Dermatol. 2011;164(1):154-62. https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/tomato-paste-rich-in-lycopene-protects-against-cutaneous-photodam/
   - 20 women, 55 g tomato paste (16 mg lycopene) in olive oil vs olive oil, 12 weeks: MED not significantly different between groups; erythemal D30 higher with tomato paste (P = 0.03). Supports "small effect, not a sunscreen replacement". (Background only, not quoted on screen.)
Mechanism ("sparks of damage" = UV-generated free radicals / reactive oxygen species; lycopene = antioxidant carotenoid that quenches them) is the standard explanation given in sources 1 and 3; shown as a metaphor.
4. American Academy of Dermatology, "Sunscreen FAQs / stats": https://www.aad.org/media/stats-sunscreen
   - "Dermatologists recommend using a sunscreen with an SPF of at least 30, which blocks 97% of the sun's UVB rays." (→ "a daily SPF 30", "blocks 97% of UVB"). Sunscreen is topical and does not need weeks of intake to build up (contrast with the 10-week tomato effect, no difference at week 4).
Illustrative UV-dot scene: share of burning UV getting through ≈ 1/SPF (SPF 2–3 → about a third to a half; SPF 30 → about 3%, matching AAD's 97% blocked).
