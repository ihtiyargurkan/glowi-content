Can you eat your way to sun protection?

Scientists tested a surprising food. Tomato paste.

Its red pigment, lycopene, travels through your blood. When U-V hits your skin, it sets off tiny sparks of damage. Lycopene acts like a sponge for those sparks.

In a small two thousand one study, people ate forty grams of tomato paste a day, with olive oil. After ten weeks, a U-V lamp left forty percent less redness than in people who only had the oil.

Sounds big. But the lead researcher compared it to sunscreen with an S-P-F of just two or three. And at week four, there was no difference yet.

Sunscreen doesn't need weeks to work. A daily S-P-F thirty protects far more.

So enjoy tomatoes with a little oil, as a bonus. But keep sunscreen as your shield.
