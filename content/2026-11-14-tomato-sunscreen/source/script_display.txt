Can you eat your way to sun protection?

Scientists tested a surprising food. Tomato paste.

Its red pigment, lycopene, travels through your blood. When UV hits your skin, it sets off tiny sparks of damage. Lycopene acts like a sponge for those sparks.

In a small 2001 study, people ate 40 grams of tomato paste a day, with olive oil. After 10 weeks, a UV lamp left 40% less redness than in people who only had the oil.

Sounds big. But the lead researcher compared it to sunscreen with an SPF of just 2 or 3. And at week 4, there was no difference yet.

Sunscreen doesn't need weeks to work. A daily SPF 30 protects far more.

So enjoy tomatoes with a little oil, as a bonus. But keep sunscreen as your shield.
