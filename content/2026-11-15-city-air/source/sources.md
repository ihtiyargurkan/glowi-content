# Sources — city air and dark spots (opened 2026-11-09)
1. Vierkötter A, Schikowski T, Ranft U, Sugiri D, Matsui M, Krämer U, Krutmann J. Airborne particle exposure and extrinsic skin aging. J Invest Dermatol. 2010;130(12):2719-26. https://www.jidonline.org/article/S0022-202X(15)34645-5/fulltext
   - 400 Caucasian women aged 70–80, rural and urban Ruhr area, Germany (also https://www.cosmeticsdesign.com/Article/2010/11/18/Traffic-related-air-pollution-linked-to-skin-aging).
   - Abstract: "An increase in soot (per 0.5 × 10−5 per m) and particles from traffic ... was associated with 20% more pigment spots on forehead and cheeks." Results: 22% more spots on forehead and 20% more on cheeks per increase of one interquartile range of soot.
   - "Air pollution exposure was significantly correlated to extrinsic skin aging signs, in particular to pigment spots", "less pronounced to wrinkles" (only nasolabial folds significant). Observational (cross-sectional) → "a link, not proof".
2. Bouchard KV, Costin G-E. Promoting NAMs for research on skin color changes in response to environmental stress factors: tobacco and air pollution. Front Toxicol. 2023;5:1256399. https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/ftox.2023.1256399
   - "Ambient PM was shown to penetrate the skin either following the route of hair follicles and sweat ducts" (→ "slip into your pores"); PM increases ROS / oxidative stress (→ "slow chemical stress, like rust"); "PM2.5 increased melanin content" in melanocytes; AhR activation linked to melanogenesis and lentigines (→ "wake up your pigment cells").
   - Notes particle penetration "is still debatable".
3. Sunlight as a main driver of dark spots: standard dermatology guidance (AAD); used only qualitatively ("sunlight drives dark spots too").
