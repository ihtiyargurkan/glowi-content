# Sources — chocolate and breakouts (opened 2026-11-09)
1. Fulton JE, Plewig G, Kligman AM. Effect of chocolate on acne vulgaris. JAMA. 1969 — summarised in source 2 and https://www.acne.org/chocolate-and-acne
   - 65 subjects; chocolate bar vs a chocolate-free "placebo" bar with 28% vegetable fat that tried to mimic the lipid and sugar content of the chocolate bar; no significant difference (→ "found no link", "fake bar loaded with fat and sugar too"). Industry-sponsored (acne.org).
2. Caperton C, Block S, Viera M, Keri J, Berman B. Double-blind, placebo-controlled study assessing the effect of chocolate consumption in subjects with a history of acne vulgaris. J Clin Aesthet Dermatol. 2014. https://jcadonline.com/double-blind-placebo-controlled-study-assessing-the-effect-of-chocolate-consumption-in-subjects-with-a-history-of-acne-vulgaris/
   - 14 men (13 completed), capsules of unsweetened 100% cocoa: more acne lesions at day 4 and day 7. Describes the 1969 placebo critique (Mackie & Mackie). (→ "newer studies used real cocoa", "mostly men", "small")
3. Vongraviopap S, Asawanonda P. Dark chocolate exacerbates acne. Int J Dermatol. 2016;55(5):587-91 (PubMed 26711092). Thesis abstract opened: https://cuir.car.chula.ac.th/bitstream/123456789/45753/1/5674085530.pdf ; design also at https://www.acne.org/chocolate-and-acne
   - 25 acne-prone men (18–30), 25 g of 99% dark chocolate daily for 4 weeks after a 4-week chocolate-free period; significant increases in comedones (blackheads) and inflammatory papules (red bumps) from 2 weeks (P < 0.01). No placebo/comparison group.
4. Netea SA et al. Chocolate consumption modulates cytokine production in healthy individuals. Cytokine. 2013 (PubMed 23465690). Abstract: https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/bvsvs/resource/es/mdl-23465690
   - "chocolate consumption primes human blood mononuclear cells ... to release more IL-1β and IL-10 upon stimulation with Propionibacterium acnes" → "immune cells reacted more strongly to acne bacteria" (lab study on blood cells).
