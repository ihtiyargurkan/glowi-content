Why do your fingers wrinkle in the bath?

For years, people thought your skin just soaks up water and swells. That's only the first step.

The wrinkles are made by your nerves. They tell the blood vessels under your fingertips to tighten, and the skin above them folds into ridges.

Researchers have known since the 1930s that a finger with damaged nerves doesn't wrinkle at all. Swelling alone can't explain that.

So why bother? The ridges may work like tire treads, channeling water away so you can grip.

In one test, 20 volunteers soaked their hands for 30 minutes. With wrinkled fingers, they moved wet marbles 12% faster.

So pruney fingers aren't damage. They're your nerves doing their job. After a long soak, pat dry and moisturize.
