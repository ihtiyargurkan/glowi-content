Is your phone aging your skin?

Blue light serums say yes. But a 2022 review found that blue light from screens is up to 1,000 times weaker than the blue light in sunlight.

Picture two buckets. The sun fills one in seconds. Your phone drips into the other, up to 1,000 times slower.

In one study, people with melasma held a bright screen 20 centimeters from their dark patches, 8 hours a day for 5 days. The patches did not get worse.

A review of device studies found no clear evidence that screen light harms skin.

The real source is the sky. Visible light can darken skin, and in deeper skin tones those marks lasted longer than a UVA tan.

So skip the screen panic and protect against the sun. If dark spots are your worry, look for a tinted sunscreen, because its pigments can block visible light.
