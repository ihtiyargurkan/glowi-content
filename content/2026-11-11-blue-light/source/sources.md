# Sources — screen blue light and skin (opened 2026-11-04)
1. Lim HW, Kohli I, Ruvolo E, Kolbe L, Hamzavi IH. Impact of visible light on skin health: the role of antioxidants and free radical quenchers in skin protection. J Am Acad Dermatol. 2022;86(3 Suppl). https://core.ac.uk/download/524796407.pdf
   - Blue light (420–490 nm) from screens, TVs and phones is "99 to 1000 times less than that of sunlight" → "up to 1,000 times weaker". Summarises Duteil et al.: a high-intensity screen 8 h/day for 5 days at 20 cm from melasma lesions "did not worsen melasma lesions".
2. Duteil L, et al. Short-term exposure to blue light emitted by electronic devices does not worsen melasma. J Am Acad Dermatol. 2020;83(3):913-914. https://scispace.com/papers/short-term-exposure-to-blue-light-emitted-by-electronic-3s10jwcfgv (record opened; details via source 1)
3. Ceresnie MS, Patel J, Lim HW, Kohli I. The cutaneous effects of blue light from electronic devices: a systematic review with health hazard identification. Photochem Photobiol Sci. 2022. https://scholarlycommons.henryford.com/dermatology_articles/715
   - 9 studies included; no hazard identified for pigmentation, erythema or melasma; not classified as a photoaging hazard; low confidence evidence → "no clear evidence that screen light harms skin".
4. Mahmoud BH, et al. Impact of long-wavelength UVA and visible light on melanocompetent skin. J Invest Dermatol. 2010;130(8):2092-7. https://api-pubmed-ncbi.laibokeji.com/20410914
   - 20 volunteers; visible light induced pigmentation in skin types IV–VI that was darker and more sustained than UVA1-induced pigmentation; none in type II → "in deeper skin tones those marks lasted longer than a UVA tan".
5. Eadie E, et al. Commercial visible-light protecting sunscreens for photosensitive individuals. Br J Dermatol. 2023 (ljac112). https://discovery.dundee.ac.uk/ws/files/96275705/ljac112.pdf
   - "most sunscreens are not formulated to protect against visible wavelengths, which require VL-blocking pigments" → "a tinted sunscreen, because its pigments can block visible light".
